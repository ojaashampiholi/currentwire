Bombay HC takes suo motu cognisance of festival noise, questions midnight loudspeaker permissions

Bench flags firecracker toxicity as prima facie concern and lists matter for Tuesday replies from State, MPCB and BMC

First filed: 22 Sep 2026, 4:12 AM IST · Last updated: 22 Sep 2026, 4:12 AM IST

A Bombay High Court bench of Justices G.S. Kulkarni and Neela Gokhale on Monday 21 September 2026 took suo motu cognisance of severe noise and related air pollution from loudspeakers, amplifiers, DJs and firecrackers during festival processions, including Ganesh celebrations. The division bench expressed concern over ground-level failures to implement noise-control regulations and initiated a public interest litigation. In its order the court said there cannot be objection to festivities and processions being held, but they need to be conducted in a manner cognizant of citizens' rights and the adverse impact of high-decibel noise. It underlined that ailing senior citizens, students, children and others are left to suffer silently when celebrations breach legal rights. The court also stated that no responsible citizen can be permitted to pollute the atmosphere, including by creating noise pollution through firecrackers that enhance air toxicity.

The bench questioned the logic of permissions allowing loud music in processions until midnight when, it said it had been informed, permission for such processions with musical instruments, loudspeakers and amplifiers would otherwise run only until 10 pm. That timing mismatch sits at the centre of the court's inquiry into how norms and law are actually implemented during festival peaks in Mumbai. Notices have been issued to the State government, the Maharashtra Pollution Control Board and the Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation. The court sought the presence of Advocate General Milind Sathe, appearing with additional government pleader P.N. Diwan for the State, and directed reply affidavits addressing the issues raised. The matter was listed for Tuesday 22 September 2026.

On firecrackers, the bench said that considering the severity of air pollution in Mumbai, prima facie it is high time that firecrackers causing noise and air pollution and leading to toxicity of the air are banned so as to nurture a healthy environment. That formulation is a prima facie observation and a contemplation of a ban, not a final prohibition already in force. Reporting and public discussion should keep that distinction clear: the court has flagged the issue and sought state responses, not issued a conclusive statewide firecracker ban in the Monday order. The same order ties cracker use to both noise and air-quality harms rather than treating them as separate regulatory silos.

Separately, another Bombay High Court bench is seized of a Pune-linked public interest petition seeking time-bound enforcement of the court's 2016 noise-control judgment, including a plea for an independent third-party monitoring agency. Bar and Bench reported that senior advocate Aspi Chinoy and advocate Anand Pai have been appointed amici curiae in that matter, which is listed for early October, with a reported hearing date of 5 October. The suo motu festival-noise case and the Pune enforcement PIL are related in subject matter but procedurally distinct. Tuesday's listing of the suo motu matter will turn on the State's affidavit and the responses from MPCB and BMC on loudspeaker, DJ and firecracker controls during ongoing and upcoming festival processions.
