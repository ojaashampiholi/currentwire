US to reopen Greenland base sites under Arctic security deal set for UN signing

Narsarsuaq and Mestersvig named in sources account as Denmark, Greenland and Washington prepare Tuesday ceremony

First filed: 22 Sep 2026, 4:12 AM IST · Last updated: 22 Sep 2026, 4:12 AM IST
CurrentWire · paraphrased from packet sources
In briefThe United States plans to reopen the former Cold War base at Narsarsuaq in southern Greenland and establish a military presence at Mestersvig on the east coast under an Arctic security agreement with Denmark and Greenland due to be signed at UN headquarters in New York at 10:30 a.m. ET on Tuesday 22 September 2026. The text is not public and details are not final until signing. Denmark says Arctic security under the deal would substantially sit with NATO rather than only Washington and Copenhagen. Danish and Greenlandic statements stress the arrangement does not cede sovereignty. Only Pituffik Space Base remains an active US installation in Greenland today.

The United States plans to reopen a former Cold War military base in southern Greenland and establish a military presence at an east coast site under an Arctic security agreement that Denmark, Greenland and Washington are due to sign at United Nations headquarters in New York on Tuesday, Reuters reported, citing three people familiar with the matter. The southern site is Narsarsuaq, once the US Bluie West One facility closed in the 1950s and now home to only a few dozen residents. The east coast location is Mestersvig, an outpost currently used by Denmark's Sirius Dog Sled Patrol, a special forces unit, according to two of those people. The signing is scheduled for 10:30 a.m. ET, or 1430 GMT, on Tuesday 22 September 2026. The text of the agreement has not been made public, and the people familiar with the deal said its details are not finalized until it is signed.

Danish officials have framed the arrangement as placing Arctic security substantially under NATO rather than leaving it only to Washington and Copenhagen. Danish Foreign Minister Lars Lokke Rasmussen told national broadcaster TV2 that responsibility for security in the Arctic would not be only Danish or American and that to a large extent NATO would look after the Arctic. A NATO spokesperson said the alliance welcomed an agreement that would help advance security, stability and cooperation in the region. Rasmussen has said Copenhagen is open to accommodating the United States' legitimate security interests while declining to discuss the contents before the signing. Key points that remain undisclosed include the scale of any buildup and whether the deal amends the 1951 defense agreement that underpins the existing US presence in Greenland.

Danish and Greenlandic statements have stressed that the deal does not cede sovereignty. Greenland's Prime Minister Jens-Frederik Nielsen has said the proposed arrangement recognizes the sovereignty and territorial integrity of the Kingdom and the right of the Greenlandic people to self-determination. Danish Foreign Minister Rasmussen has spoken of a binding agreement that strengthens Arctic and North Atlantic security within NATO and Europe while respecting the Kingdom's red lines. President Donald Trump has described related arrangements in terms of US control over Greenland's security, including language about permanent control and requirements for written US approval before any adversary presence. Copenhagen has not endorsed that phrasing. Coverage should keep Trump's description distinct from Danish and Greenlandic wording.

During the Cold War the United States maintained 17 military installations and more than 10,000 personnel in Greenland. Today only Pituffik Space Base remains an active US installation on the island, with some 150 US military personnel, according to Reuters. The planned return to Narsarsuaq and a presence at Mestersvig would mark the first named expansion of US basing footprints beyond Pituffik under the forthcoming trilateral deal. Governments of Denmark and Greenland did not immediately respond to Reuters requests for comment on the base details when the report published, and the White House likewise did not immediately respond. Until the New York ceremony and any published text, the operational scope, legal instruments and force posture linked to the two sites remain provisional.
