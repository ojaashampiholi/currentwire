A Supreme Court Bench led by Justice Bhuyan held that arrests which fail to inform the accused of the grounds of arrest in a familiar language, or which deny counsel of choice, violate Article 22(1) of the Constitution. Such arrests, the Court said, must end in immediate release.

The Hindu reported that the Bench further held that filing a chargesheet does not cure an Article 22 defect. If police seek to take the person back into custody, they need Magistrate sanction before any re-arrest. The ruling targets a pattern in which procedural lapses at the moment of arrest are later covered by investigative paperwork.

For accused persons, the judgment tightens the link between arrest formality and liberty. For police and prosecutors, it raises the cost of shortcuts at arrest and inserts a judicial gate before a second custody cycle. Trial courts are likely to see more release applications citing the Bhuyan Bench standard.

Watch how High Courts and Magistrates apply the sanction requirement in pending custody disputes, and whether investigating agencies issue fresh arrest-protocol guidance. Implementation at the station and Magistrate levels will determine how far the ruling travels beyond the headline.
