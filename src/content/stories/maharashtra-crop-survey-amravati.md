Maharashtra has started early crop-loss inspections in drought-hit belts even as a formal drought declaration remains pending, the Times of India reported from Nagpur. Revenue Minister Chandrashekhar Bawankule said surveys are under way and that a decision on declaration would come soon. The sequencing matters for farmers and for administrators: field assessment is moving first, while the statewide administrative call has not yet been issued, and overstating a declaration that has not happened would misread the current legal and fiscal posture. TOI's Nagpur desk places the early survey push squarely in the drought-affected geography rather than as a finished statewide finding.

Agriculture Minister Dattatray Bharne said the state would seek maximum compensation for affected farmers and pressed for prompt, transparent inspections across Vidarbha and Marathwada, according to the same TOI coverage. Free Press Journal and India Today have separately reported that Chief Minister Devendra Fadnavis framed the drought assessment in stages and stressed that relief measures are already under way. Officials are treating the exercise as both an aid pipeline and a documentation process ahead of any central claim, which is why survey quality and speed are being discussed together. Bharne's public line on maximum compensation also signals that the agriculture department wants the eventual claim package to reflect the full assessed loss rather than a truncated count.

In Amravati, Collector Ashish Yerekar ordered key field officers to remain at headquarters around the clock under the Disaster Management Act 2005, prioritising crop assessment, loan restructuring, and farmer help desks, TOI reported. That local directive puts continuous staffing behind the survey push in one of the worst-hit districts and keeps farmer-facing counters open while inspections run. Loan restructuring and help desks sit alongside crop assessment because cash-flow stress often hits before a formal drought notification arrives, and the collector's order treats those channels as part of the same operational response rather than as later add-ons.

Fadnavis has said formal NDRF-style panchnamas will begin from 5 October and that relief need not wait for a formal drought declaration, India Today and Free Press Journal reported. The chief minister's line separates immediate state aid from the later paperwork track used for national disaster response claims, and Free Press Journal's account of a three-stage assessment matches that staged approach. As of the latest filings, Maharashtra has not announced a statewide drought declaration; early surveys, Amravati's round-the-clock field posture, and the 5 October panchnama timeline are the concrete steps on record. Any subsequent declaration would be a distinct administrative act, not something already completed by the start of inspections.
