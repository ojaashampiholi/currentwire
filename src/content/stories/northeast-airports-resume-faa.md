By Monday evening US East Coast time, flights were resuming at major Northeast airports after a failed primary communications circuit at the Philadelphia Terminal Radar Approach Control facility and a backup fiber-optic cut near rail construction works involving Amtrak and New Jersey Transit corridors. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said telecom lines had been repaired and all Northeast airport operations were resuming, Reuters reported. The Associated Press said operations were resuming at John F. Kennedy, LaGuardia, Newark Liberty and Philadelphia airports, with significant delays still expected through the rest of the day. Earlier, FlightAware figures cited in ABC Australia's account pointed to thousands of delayed or cancelled flights amid arrivals for the United Nations General Assembly in New York.

Duffy blamed an Amtrak construction crew for the fiber cut; Amtrak pointed to a New Jersey Transit crew working between Newark and New Brunswick. That attribution dispute is unresolved in the cited reporting. Controllers had lost radar and communications when the primary circuit failed and the backup fiber was found severed, AP reported. Recovery is under way, but travelers still face cascading delays, schedule uncertainty and airline change policies rather than a return to full operational normality across the Northeast system.
