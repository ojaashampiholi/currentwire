Sri Lanka's Colombo Special Three-Judge High Court was due to begin delivering its verdict at 9:30 am local time on Tuesday 22 September in the main trial of 24 men accused over the 2019 Easter Sunday bombings. AFP reporting carried by RFI said the case is the first time defendants face sentencing for direct involvement in the attacks on churches and hotels. Prosecutors filed 23,270 charges, covering terrorism, conspiracy, murder and explosives offences. Local Newswire named the bench as Navaratne Marasinghe, Sujeewa Nishshanka and Ramanadan Kannan. Police said special security would surround the court complex for the hearing. The judgment result was not available by this desk's 08:05 IST cutoff and is therefore still pending.

Among those on trial is Naufer Maulavi, listed by Newswire among defendants charged with aiding Zahran Hashim's suicide squad. All of the accused deny involvement. Wire tallies for the death toll differ slightly and should be kept attributed: RFI cites 279 dead, including 45 foreigners, while Newswire cites 274 dead, including about 40 foreigners and 35 children. Newswire's advance note dated 21 September confirmed delivery for 22 September; RFI's AFP report entered the morning IST window on verdict day itself. Separate controversies over alleged state or intelligence links remain a parallel track and should not be merged into this verdict lead without clear attribution. The courtroom process described here is the main criminal trial of the 24 defendants.

The scale of the indictment, more than twenty-three thousand counts, reflects how prosecutors framed conspiracy, murder and explosives offences across the coordinated attacks. AFP and RFI present the day as a landmark accountability step because it is the first sentencing window for defendants charged with direct involvement. Capital punishment remains legally possible in Sri Lanka for the most serious offences, but long-standing practice has seen death sentences commute rather than carried out. That legal context belongs in the background as a careful note on sentencing range; it is not a prediction of any sentence the bench may or may not impose once the verdict is read.

What remains open is the content of the judgment itself. Delivery was scheduled to start after 9:30 am local time, which falls after this packet's filing window. Mid-morning continuing coverage should track whether convictions, acquittals or mixed findings are announced, and on which of the charged counts. Until that public result is issued, any forecast of outcome would be speculation and is avoided here. This draft covers only the verdict-day setup: schedule, charge scale, named bench, defendant posture, security posture and attributed casualty figures from RFI and Newswire.
