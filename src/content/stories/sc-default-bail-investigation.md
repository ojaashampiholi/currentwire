The Supreme Court on Monday 21 September 2026 allowed Maharashtra's appeal in a 2022 Mumbai Anti-Terrorism Squad case under the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, restoring an order that gave investigators another 15 days. A bench of Justices Prashant Kumar Mishra and Shree Chandrashekhar set aside a July 2024 Bombay High Court judgment that had invalidated the extension and granted default bail. The Supreme Court instead restored the special judge's order of 18 January 2023 and dismissed the accused persons' default bail application dated 20 January 2023. The decision is reported as 2026 INSC 1021.

The dispute turned on how the prosecution's request for more investigation time should be read. One statement in that request said sufficient material had been collected to file a chargesheet. The High Court treated that statement as showing the investigation was complete, undermining the basis for granting an additional 15 days. The Supreme Court rejected an isolated reading. It noted that the same application said more time was needed to analyse scientific and electronic material and to furnish a complete report. Read as a whole, the request did not amount to an admission that no investigative work remained. The extension at issue was a limited 15-day period, not an open-ended continuation. That assessment led the bench to revive the special judge's extension and remove the procedural basis on which default bail had been granted.

The holding is limited to the extension request and the resulting default bail claim. The bench said a prosecution statement that enough material exists for a chargesheet does not by itself establish that the investigation has ended when the request also identifies analysis and reporting that remain unfinished. It also said criminal investigation ordinarily falls within the police domain and that courts cannot direct a probe to stop unless exceptional facts or a statutory bar justify intervention. That principle does not confer unlimited authority on investigators. In this case, it supported the conclusion that the request for 15 additional days had to be considered in full rather than cut down to a single sentence about the material already collected.

The underlying allegations connected to the Popular Front of India and the ATS case remain allegations. The Supreme Court did not determine guilt; its ruling addresses the time allowed for investigation and whether default bail followed from the earlier extension decision. Respondent Momin Moiuddin Gulam Hasan, also identified as Moin Mistri, must surrender within 30 days of the judgment. The court left him free to apply for regular bail, which would be a separate request considered on its own basis. The immediate effect is therefore procedural: the January 2023 extension stands, the January 2023 default bail application is dismissed, and the respondent's ability to seek regular bail remains open.
