Mumbai Crime Branch investigators are treating newly described closed-circuit camera recordings from IIT Bombay as central to the probe into the death of second-year student Sahil Wakode. The account of what those cameras show comes from The Indian Express, updated at 07:34 IST on Tuesday 22 September and attributed to police sources. According to that report, footage from roughly 12:30 pm to 12:45 pm on the day of the exam is said to show Professor Suryanarayana Doolla questioning Wakode after he appeared to look at something in his pocket, then taking him aside. Later footage is said to show Wakode entering his hostel room around 1:25 pm. This desk has not independently viewed the recordings. The CCTV description is carried by a single outlet and should be read with that limitation in mind.

The Crime Branch team led by ACP Dharamveer Bansod is expected to analyse the footage as part of the continuing inquiry. On Monday the same team searched Wakode's hostel room and conducted a panchnama. Officials told the Express that no suicide note had been found so far and that no summons had yet been issued while paperwork is still being collected from Powai police. The institutional focus of this update is therefore investigative process: what the cameras are said to capture, who will review that material, and what procedural steps have and have not been taken. It does not revisit earlier campus protest coverage as fresh news.

The first information report, based on a complaint by Wakode's father Ravindra, names Doolla among others under abetment to suicide provisions and relevant sections of the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act. Those caste-discrimination and abetment claims remain allegations under investigation. Nothing in the available reporting establishes guilt, and this draft does not treat ChatGPT use, exam misconduct, or any finding of discrimination as settled fact. The Express update supplies the CCTV narrative that investigators are now weighing; it does not replace the need for forensic and documentary work already under way.

What to watch next is the Crime Branch analysis of the exam-hall and hostel recordings, any further process served once Powai paperwork is in hand, and whether additional independent reporting corroborates the camera chronology. Until that analysis and any charging decisions are complete, the probe remains open and developing. Readers should treat the single-source CCTV description as provisional reporting attributed to police sources via the Express, not as a court-tested record of events.
