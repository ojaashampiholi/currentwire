The White House said President Donald Trump and First Lady Melania Trump will host Xi Jinping, President of the People's Republic of China, and Madame Peng Liyuan for an official state visit centred on Thursday 24 September 2026. First Lady Melania Trump's office released the ceremonial details ahead of the visit, setting out arrival, honour, cultural, and farewell elements across three days. China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs separately confirmed that Xi will visit the United States from 23 to 25 September at Trump's invitation, Reuters reported. The dual confirmation from Washington and Beijing fixes the window even while the public text remains focused on protocol rather than bargaining agendas. That split between confirmed dates and unreleased deal language is the right way to read the first lady's briefing at this stage.

On 23 September, the Chinese guests are to be greeted at Joint Base Andrews on arrival. The White House framing treats that stop as the start of the formal programme rather than the centrepiece day, a standard pattern for state visits that open with a military airfield welcome before moving to White House grounds. Logistics and honour details for Andrews were included in the first lady's release alongside the broader three-day outline, giving planners and press a fixed first waypoint for the itinerary.

Thursday 24 September carries the core state-visit ceremonies: a South Lawn arrival with full military honours, remarks, a visit by the first ladies to the Smithsonian's National Museum of Asian Art, and an East Room state dinner. Those events are the public face of the visit as described by the White House and will dominate visual coverage. They do not, by themselves, disclose what will be negotiated in bilateral talks on trade, security, or other files. Distinguishing the ceremonial card from negotiating outcomes is essential; a full military welcome and a state dinner are protocol facts, not substitutes for announced agreements.

On 25 September the schedule includes tea in the Red Room and a stop at the National Archives before farewell. Reuters' confirmation of the 23 to 25 September window aligns with the White House calendar and closes the public itinerary as currently released. The published programme is a ceremonial itinerary. Negotiating outcomes remain separate from the pageantry listed so far, and CurrentWire will treat any later deal announcements as distinct developments rather than as implied by the honour schedule alone. Until those announcements appear, the White House and Reuters records support only the ceremonial timeline summarised here.
