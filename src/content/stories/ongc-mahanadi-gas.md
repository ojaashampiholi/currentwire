Oil and Natural Gas Corporation has reported a natural gas find at MDW-28 (also identified as MN-DW18-1-H-D), the first well in its deepwater Mahanadi Offshore campaign roughly 43 km off Konark on the Odisha coast, the Times of India and TOI Business reported. The well was perforated on 18 September 2026 after geological and geophysical analysis, and ONGC said the early results showed encouraging gas flow with stable reservoir pressure. That combination of flow response and pressure behaviour is what explorers watch first when deciding whether a deepwater hit deserves a fuller appraisal programme. The strike is being framed across official and company messaging as an exploration success, not the start of commercial production, and none of the reporting treats MDW-28 as an immediate addition to India's producing gas slate.

Petroleum and Natural Gas Minister Hardeep Singh Puri had spudded the well on 25 July under the Samudra Manthan programme and later described the outcome as a milestone for the basin. According to coverage in the Times of India and Hindu Business Line, the minister indicated that the find, taken together with nearby discoveries, could support cluster development under the Petroleum and Natural Gas Rules 2025. Cluster logic matters in deepwater because shared facilities can change the economics of several modest hits that would not stand alone. Even so, that pathway remains contingent on further technical work rather than a near-term output decision, and the ministry's framing has stayed careful about not equating a first-well result with a field development sanction.

Hindu Business Line reported that the gas strike interval sits between 1,441 and 1,452 metres, with the well drilled to a total depth of 1,623 metres. Those figures place the hit in a relatively tight vertical window within the drilled section and will feed the next round of reservoir characterisation, including how continuous the pay may be and how the interval connects to surrounding geology. ONGC and ministry statements have stressed that the campaign is still in its early deepwater phase off Odisha, with MDW-28 serving as the opening data point rather than a finished picture of the block. TOI's Bhubaneswar and business desks both locate the well in the same offshore corridor off Konark, reinforcing the geographic consistency of the find.

Further appraisal drilling and commercial evaluation are still required before any development plan can be locked in. Officials have not announced production timelines, reserve estimates suitable for investment decisions, or a timeline for first gas, and reporting across TOI and Hindu Business Line is clear that MDW-28 remains a discovery well pending fuller assessment. For now, the result strengthens the case for continued work in the Mahanadi deepwater acreage under Samudra Manthan without changing the status of India's near-term gas supply. Readers should treat subsequent appraisal results, not this first perforation response, as the next material checkpoint.
