A large section of IIT Bombay faculty held a peace march on Monday in support of Prof Suryanarayana Doolla, who has been named in an FIR linked to the death of second-year student Sahil Wakode on 18 September, the Times of India and The Hindu reported. Marchers carried banners seeking justice for Doolla and questioning consequences before evidence is tested, a framing that TOI summarised as concern about damage to reputation ahead of completed inquiry. The demonstration unfolded as the Mumbai Crime Branch leads the probe into the case. Allegations remain under investigation, and no finding of culpability has been established in court. This report does not treat contested claims as proven and does not describe the manner of the student's death.

The IIT Bombay Faculty Forum said Doolla remains a faculty member of the institute and was only temporarily relieved as Dean (Administrative Affairs) to allow an independent probe. He has not been suspended from the institute, according to the Forum's position reported by The Hindu and TOI. That distinction between administrative relief and institute-wide suspension has become central to how faculty supporters are framing his status while the inquiry proceeds. The Hindu's account pairs the Forum's backing with confirmation that Crime Branch has taken charge of the investigation, keeping institutional employment status and criminal process on separate tracks in the public record.

Some students backed due process at an open house on campus, The Statesman and TOI reported, even as other student voices have protested the faculty march itself. The IIT Madras Faculty Association urged restraint until the inquiry concludes, adding an external faculty voice that prioritises waiting for evidence over public mobilisation. Separately, a dissenting IIT Bombay professor said the Faculty Forum does not speak for all faculty, underlining that opinion on campus is not uniform and that Monday's march, however large, should not be read as a unanimous institutional verdict.

Coverage in The Hindu, TOI, and The Statesman converges on process rather than settled blame: Crime Branch is investigating, Doolla's dean role has been paused for that probe, and he continues as faculty pending further steps. Claims circulating around the case, including any caste-related allegations, have not been proven and should be treated as contested matters before the investigation.
