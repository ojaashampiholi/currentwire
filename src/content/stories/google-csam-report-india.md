Google has agreed, in principle and as part of ongoing discussions with the Government of India, to a proposal under which India-linked cases of child sexual abuse material flagged on its platforms would be reported directly to Indian authorities. Reuters reported the development from Bengaluru on 21 September. The Hindu published the same storyline at 07:51 IST on Tuesday 22 September and updated it at 08:21 IST. Across those accounts the story is an agreement and a plan to operationalise sharing with domestic agencies, not confirmation that a finished live reporting feed is already running between Google and Indian police.

The proposed path would break from the common global practice of routing such flags first through the US-based non-profit National Center for Missing and Exploited Children (NCMEC) before domestic agencies receive them. Indian government sources told Reuters they had pressed Google and Microsoft to report India-linked cases directly because the existing chain delays police action once material is flagged. That pressure mirrors last week's focus on Meta, which CNBC-TV18 cited as the immediate backdrop when it reported Google's proposal. A Google spokesperson said the company invests to detect, deter, remove and report such content, and described the proposal as part of continuing talks with the government rather than a closed, one-off deal.

On the operational side, CNBC-TV18 reported that Google proposed information-sharing with the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) under the Ministry of Home Affairs, in coordination with the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY). Sharing is expected once a technical framework is ready. That timing caveat is central to accurate coverage: what the outlets describe today is pathway design, accountability for India-linked cases, and the intent to shorten the reporting chain, not an assertion that Indian authorities already receive a live Google feed under the new model.

What to watch next is whether and when that technical framework is completed, how I4C and MeitY define intake and follow-up for platform reports, and whether Microsoft or other large platforms take parallel steps under the same government pressure. Until the framework is ready and publicly confirmed as operational, this remains a proposed change in reporting pathway and timing.
